leftist heap merge

Merge the right paths, then restore the leftist property from the bottom up.

Each node shows its value and rank. A/B identify the original heap. L/R mark child edges. Trees scale to fit the screen. Orange rings mark the current operation; dashed rings mean the rank is pending repair.

How the merge works

A min-leftist heap has two rules: each parent is no larger than its children, and rank(left) ≥ rank(right). Here an empty subtree has rank 0 and a leaf has rank 1; otherwise rank(node) = 1 + min(rank(left), rank(right)).

  1. Choose the smaller current root and append it to the merged right path. Its left subtree stays attached. Repeat until one right path ends, then attach the remainder.
  2. Walk back up the merged path. Check the child ranks, swap children if the left rank is smaller, then update the node's rank. Each action is its own step.

The intermediate tree can temporarily violate the leftist property. Dashed nodes retain their old ranks until the update step. Selecting the smaller root is distinct from swapping a node’s children.
